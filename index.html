Reading a shot backwards: reverse-engineering footage you did not shoot

Watching a good shot tells you it works. It does not tell you why, and "why" is the only part you can reuse. Reverse-engineering footage is the skill of turning admiration into a shot list: identifying the camera, the light, the lens choice, and the production method behind each frame, and writing it down in a form you can reproduce.

Start with the camera. Is it locked off, handheld, on a gimbal, or on a slider? A locked-off frame with a slow digital push is not the same shot as a physical dolly move, even when the final image looks similar, and the difference decides how you recreate it. Then find the lens: wide angles stretch faces at the edges, long lenses compress the background into the subject. Both are readable from the image if you look at what happens to the corners.

Light next. Count the sources you can see evidence of: a key from one side, a rim separating the subject from the background, practical lights in frame. Note the contrast ratio rather than the brightness — the ratio is what gives a scene its mood, and it survives being re-lit on a different day.

Then the part most people never write down: how the shot was probably made. Live action with a real camera, a 3D render, an AI-generated clip, or a screen recording. The tells are different for each — AI footage has a particular relationship between hair and background, renders have a clean depth of field that never breathes, and real footage carries sensor noise that no compressor fully hides.

The output of all this is a remake plan: a shot list with framing, movement, light, and a suggested generation prompt for each shot. That is a document someone else can execute, which is the whole point. An analysis nobody can act on is a film review.

Doing this by hand for a ten-shot sequence takes an afternoon and a lot of pausing. It is also exactly the task Rea Video automates: it breaks footage down shot by shot and returns a structured breakdown plus a remake plan, running locally, with exports as text or JSON.

Reverse-engineering is not copying. It is learning the vocabulary of the shots you already admire, so the next thing you make starts from a decision instead of a guess.
